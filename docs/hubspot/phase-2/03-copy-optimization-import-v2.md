**Variant:** Import-focused, pre-release
**Revision:** v2 — copy-optimized pass. Structure, sections, visual guidance, and implementation notes preserved from the source; ship-ready copy tightened across every section for concision, specificity, and conversion.

This pass applies PAS framing at the page level (hero promise → category slot → sharpened problem → solution + aha → proof → differentiation → objection handling → final CTA). It respects the source doc's voice constraint — *precise, understated, no exclamation points, no motivational-quote energy*. Urgency comes from specificity ("opens in weeks", "the first compare", "before the public list"), not from adjective inflation. Where the copywriting prompt asked for "emotional triggers," this pass delivers them via the visitor's own second-person recognition beats, not via hype.

Global design constants remain unchanged from the source (graph-paper grid, dark/light modes, iOS blue accent, waitlist-only CTAs with varied button labels, no App Store badges anywhere). See the source doc for full implementation of those; this document carries the optimized copy and diffs the visual/implementation notes only where copy changes require it.

## Section 7 — FAQ

**Full copy (optimized).**

- **Section H2:**
  > Answers to what you're probably thinking.

- **Item 1 — When can I use it?**
  > Early access opens this fall, in waves. Waitlist first. We'll email you a TestFlight link when it's your turn — you're not obligated to accept. Two emails total: one when your invite is ready, one at launch. No drip.

- **Item 2 — How does the import work? Will it grab photos I don't want it to?**
  > Two ways, both on your terms. Lots of photos: group them into a Photos album, then import the whole album in one tap. A few photos: pick them out of your camera roll one at a time. Recomp doesn't scan or auto-detect anything — you decide what belongs. Nothing leaves your phone.

- **Item 3 — What if my old photos aren't consistent — different poses, lighting, distance?**
  > That's most people's camera roll, and it's fine. The compare view has alignment tools — nudge, scale, match reference points — so the delta reads honestly. Recomp doesn't fix bad inputs. It makes uneven inputs comparable in a way a bare album never will.

- **Item 4 — Isn't this just an iPhone album?**
  > An album is a folder. Recomp is a timeline. The difference is compare — any two dates, aligned and dated, side by side, no hunting. Try it once on your imported library. The gap is obvious.

- **Item 5 — Do I have to track weight, workouts, or macros?**
  > No. Weight and notes are optional on every check-in. Workouts and macros aren't in Recomp — those tools already exist and are good. Recomp handles the piece they skip.

- **Item 6 — How much will it cost?**
  > Free to start. Importing your camera roll and the first stretch of compares cost nothing. Paid once you're serious — less than a month of gym coffee. Waitlist members see pricing before anything charges.

- **Item 7 — Where do my photos live? Are they private?**
  > On your phone. Recomp doesn't upload, doesn't share, doesn't publish a feed. Import and alignment happen on-device. If you back up to iCloud Photos, they go where your other photos go. Nothing else.

- **Item 8 — Do I need to keep taking photos after the import?**
  > No — the import alone is worth it. Your existing library becomes a proper timeline. Most people keep the cadence going once they've seen the compare; the [track variant](../track/) covers the weekly ritual.

- **Item 9 — I already use Hevy / MacroFactor / a spreadsheet. Do I need this?**
  > Probably yes — for a different reason. Those tools track inputs: training, food, numbers. Recomp tracks the outcome: the physique itself, over time. Missing piece, not a replacement.

- **Item 10 — iOS only?**
  > For now, yes. Recomp is iOS-native — Photos, HealthKit, the Apple design system — because that's where our first users are and where the years of camera-roll history live. Android comes once iOS is where it needs to be.

**Transitional line to the final CTA (below the accordion):**
> Still on the fence? Get on the list. The first compare from your own camera roll is what convinces you.

**Copy diff & rationale.**

- Every answer tightened by 20–40% via routine cuts: *actually, just, really, you can*, auxiliary *will/would*, and repeated re-framings of the same point.
- Item 1: promise made countable — "Two emails total: one when your invite is ready, one at launch." A number is more trust-building than the vague "we'll only email you at launch and once."
- Item 2: rewritten as parallel structure — "Lots of photos: … A few photos: …" Reads faster; the two paths become visually symmetric.
- Item 4: split the trailing "Try it once … and the gap is obvious" comma splice into two beats so the final line lands.
- Item 5: cut "and on every import" (redundant — imports become check-ins).
- Item 8: replaced the second-person "you'll want to keep the cadence going" with the third-person observation "Most people keep the cadence going once they've seen the compare" — reads as pattern, not prediction.
- Item 9: closer compressed to **"Missing piece, not a replacement"** — a callback to the Section 6 differentiation table.
- Item 10 title: "Is it just iOS?" → "iOS only?" Two words carry it.
- Transition line split into two beats — "Get on the list. The first compare…" — for rhythm and stronger tail.

**Visual guidance, CTA placement, and implementation notes:** unchanged from source. Accordion behavior, keyboard accessibility, and `prefers-reduced-motion` all preserved.
